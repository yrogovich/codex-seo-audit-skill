# Search data, competitors and authority

Use connections you are already allowed to use, or exports the user gave you. Do not ask for passwords, print credentials, assume a paid service or buy reports. Keep working with public evidence while optional access is pending.

## Search Console and analytics

- **Setup:** confirm property, hostname, protocol, timezone, dates, filters, row limits and aggregation.
- **Compare like with like:** use complete periods (for example the last 28 days against the 28 before, plus year over year). Allow for seasonality, device and country mix, brand versus non-brand, and tracking changes. Label partial days and thin data.
- **Pages and queries:** look at clicks, impressions, CTR and position with their denominators. Do not average averages: CTR is clicks divided by impressions. Query data may skip anonymized rows and may not add up to page totals. Do not blame titles for a CTR drop before checking position and query mix.
- **Indexing data:** use inspection data to tell the declared canonical, Google's chosen canonical and the real indexed state apart. Keep inspection times. A live test is not an index observation, and a `site:` search is not a reliable index count.
- **Conversions:** connect organic landing traffic to defined conversions where tracking allows. Users, sessions, events and key events differ, and platforms use different attribution, so do not force them to match. A tracking failure is a measurement problem.
- **Traffic changes:** a drop that coincides with a Google update is a hypothesis. Check the official update history, your releases, seasonality and demand before claiming a cause.

## Keywords and competitors

- Pick a few commercially meaningful themes from the real page intents. Look at current results in the target language and place, and record the query, engine, locale, date and device.
- Name the competing URLs and page types. Business competitors and search competitors can differ.
- Map intents to existing URLs and point out real gaps and overlaps. Keep suggested themes apart from measured demand.
- Report search volume, difficulty, rankings or market share only when a data source gives them, with place, period and method. Do not make up numbers. A personalized search is not a rank tracker.

## Backlinks and reputation

Use backlink data you are allowed to use, and note its index date, coverage and sample limits. Look at how relevant the linking sites are, the status of linked pages, lost links to moved pages, anchor patterns and credible mentions. Domain authority and spam scores are tool opinions, not Google scores or proof of a penalty. Do not recommend mass disavowal, buying links or automated outreach. With no backlink data, say the profile cannot be assessed.

## Repeat audits

- Keep the baseline and current evidence unchanged, and match URL mapping, samples, device, tool, period, filters and thresholds.
- Report coverage changes separately from real improvements.
- For each earlier finding, record resolved, persistent, new or unverified, with current evidence. A measurement you could not take is not a resolution.
- Retest fixes right away. Watch search results over a period you choose, and never promise a fixed time to recover rankings.
